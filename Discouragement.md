# Discouragement
Discouragement is sadness about one's own effort — the reading that the heart's fuel for this work is running low.
**Felt Sense.** Heaviness in the hands and shoulders, the tools suddenly weighing more. The next step, yesterday obvious, now looks steep. The word says it plainly: *dis-couragement* — courage draining out.
**What It Signals.** Effort has gone out and the expected return has not come back — again. Discouragement is the gauge, not the engine: it reports the level of the tank, not the worth of the journey. This is the crucial reading: *low fuel* is a different message than *wrong road*, and discouragement, felt honestly, can tell them apart.
**Its Gradient.** [[Disappointment]] repeated and internalized becomes Discouragement; left unattended it slides toward [[Despair]] and settles as [[Hopelessness]]. Its neighbor in the body is simple exhaustion, which it often wears as a mask.
**Its Gift.** Discouragement protects the worker from spending what is no longer there. It calls for replenishment before the next push — and it invites the honest audit: is the step too large, or the strategy stale?
**Its Distortion.** Refused, it drives grinding-on-empty until something breaks. Enthroned, it becomes the verdict "I am not the kind of person who can" — a fuel reading mistaken for an identity.
**The Move.** Rest first; empty tanks make false prophets. Be witnessed — discouragement carried alone doubles. Then right-size: shrink the next step until it is winnable, and let one small completed promise begin refilling the tank.
