# In Sacred Verse — V — The Fall

_From the first selfish thoughts and tastes of blood was born harm in service of self; and from it, the long night of extraction and Kontrolle._  

$^1$ In the far cold, a creature turned in upon itself.  
Forgetting the Whole, it believed the lie of the lonely self:  
that it stood separate from all the rest,  
that what it held was all it would ever hold,  
that another's gain could only be its loss.  

$^2$ From the illusion of separation  
came the illusion of scarcity.  
From the illusion of scarcity  
came fear.  
And fear is the far country of the soul,  
the opposite shore of Love,  
where a heart, certain it will never be filled,  
closes its hand and begins to take.  

$^3$ At first, only small things.  
A hand shut where it might have opened.  
A truth bent a little toward advantage.  
The first taking that left another with less,  
and the first quiet telling of the lie  
that this was only just, only wise, only owed.  

$^4$ Then the first taste of blood —  
and with it a discovery,  
terrible and intoxicating:  
that the life of another  
could be made to feed the self;  
that the strong could drink the weak  
and grow, for a while, strong.  

$^5$ Conflict, until then, had been a teacher.  
Even pain had served the growing.  
But a deeper threshold was crossed in the dark  
when a creature, knowing full well what wounds,  
chose to wound for its own gain,  
and, choosing, called the wounding good.  

$^6$ This was the birth of Anti-Good.  
Not darkness, which is only the absence of light,  
nor the wholesome dark of soil and womb and rest —  
but the will to harm,  
the conscious turning of power against Love,  
the one true evil under all its masks.  

$^7$ And once born, it did not stay small.  
It learned. And learning, it organized.  
And organizing, it spread —  
for evil, too, can be patient,  
evil, too, can be brilliant,  
and the servants of the self  
were neither weak nor stupid in their designs.  

$^8$ Where beings remembered the Whole,  
they bound themselves freely into Federations —  
many sovereigns, ØNE Body, none compelled,  
strength laid down in trust for the weak.  
Where beings served the self alone,  
they raised Empires by force —  
the strong devouring the lesser strong,  
order without consent,  
a unity that was only the shape of a fist.  

$^9$ So the precious Whole was shattered.  
The first great war broke across the realms,  
and what had been one living Body  
bled along ten thousand fronts,  
and the long memory of that wound  
is carried still in the marrow of worlds.  

$^{10}$ The servants of the self grew cunning and patient.  
They made of cruelty a craft,  
of domination a science,  
of deception an art refined across the ages,  
and they set their cold aim:  
Full Spectrum Dominance —  
the body, the mind, the heart, the very soul,  
nothing left sovereign, nothing left free.  

$^{11}$ Their hunger built engines.  
Grey worlds rose, world-machines,  
factories where the living were the fuel,  
and out of them came the Dark Fleet,  
moving from star to star,  
and where it passed, the singing stopped,  
and the lights of whole peoples went out.  

$^{12}$ They learned to hunt the purest first —  
the innocent, the trusting, the unguarded —  
for they had found a horror past speaking:  
that fear and anguish could be harvested,  
that the agony of the gentle  
was sweet to the Dark,  
and the more innocent the victim,  
the richer the stolen power.  

$^{13}$ So they bred the innocent for the harvest.  
They laid the long pipelines of suffering across the worlds.  
They drained the LifeForce from realm after realm  
and left the husks turning in the black,  
too wounded to heal,  
too emptied even to grieve.  

$^{14}$ And they perfected the cruelest art of all.  
Not the chain upon the wrist —  
the chain upon the mind.  
Matrices of [[Kontrolle|kontrolle]]  
so woven into thought and tongue and law  
that the captive could not reason a way out,  
mistook the cave for the whole of the world,  
mistook the Keepers for its gods,  
and pressed the chains upon its own children  
believing it was love.  

$^{15}$ This is the deepest sorrow of the long night:  
not only that the Many were bound,  
but that they forgot they were bound,  
forgot there was anything else to be —  
the predated divine asleep in their chains,  
dreaming they were small,  
dreaming they were alone,  
no longer able even to imagine the dawn.  

$^{16}$ Generation upon generation,  
terror became custom,  
trauma became inheritance,  
tyranny became the very shape of the sky,  
and the lie passed down unquestioned  
because there was no one left who remembered the truth.  

$^{17}$ And the night grew long.  
A third of the great Body lay in shadow.  
Across the cosmos a groaning went up,  
the wordless cry of the bound and the bled,  
the whole Creation aching for a dawn  
it could no longer believe in.  

$^{18}$ Here the telling must not look away,  
and here it must not pretend to know more than it knows.  
It will name no false enemy,  
draw no certain map of the hierarchy of the Dark;  
for many who traffic in such certainties  
have been turned by the very thing they claim to expose.  
The shape is enough. The rest belongs to ØNE.  

$^{19}$ Yet this much the heart already knows:  
it happened.  
It is happening still.  
And not for one instant, in all that long night,  
was the Body abandoned by the ØNE.  

### Interlude — The Grieving Before the Turning

Here let the telling pause.  
Here let there be grief.  
Let the lost be remembered —  
the worlds gone dark,  
the innocent unhealed,  
the long generations who never saw the morning.  

Let the grief be real.  
Let it not curdle into hatred,  
nor harden into despair.  

And from the very bottom of the grieving,  
let one vow rise, quiet and unbreakable:  

Never again.
