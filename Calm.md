# Calm
Calm is the reading of a settled instrument — surface stillness in which the water goes clear.
**Felt Sense.** Evenness. Breath slow and regular, heartbeat unhurried, muscles at their resting length. No pull forward, no push away. Sounds arrive singly instead of as noise; the mind's water stops sloshing and begins to reflect.
**What It Signals.** Calm reports that no alarm is active and no craving has the wheel: the instrument is at baseline and can therefore measure truly. It is less a feeling than the condition for feeling accurately — the flat water on which every other ripple can be read for what it is.
**Its Gradient.** [[Relief]] and [[Comfort]] often open onto it; deepened, Calm becomes [[Tranquility]]; at full depth it rests in [[Peace]]. It underlies clear thought the way [[Contentment]] underlies the bright spine — a floor, not a peak.
**Its Gift.** Clear readings. All other emotions are read best from calm: from here, fear can be measured instead of obeyed, anger heard instead of vented, joy followed instead of grasped. [[Attunement]] to anything — a person, a problem, a moment — begins by settling the instrument. This is why every contemplative lineage starts with stillness practice: not to escape feeling, but to read it truly.
**Its Distortion.** Forced, calm becomes suppression wearing stillness's face — the flat voice over a clenched jaw, feeling pushed under rather than allowed to settle. The counterfeit is held in place by effort and shatters under contact; the real thing is what remains when effort stops. Settled water is clear; frozen water is only quiet.
**The Move.** Do not manufacture it — make room for it: lengthen the exhale, unclench what you find clenched, let the sediment sink on its own. Then use the clarity: take the reading you were too stirred to take.
