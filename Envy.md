# Envy
Envy is the ache toward what another has — the self reading its own lack in the light of someone else's possession.
**Felt Sense.** A tightening at the sight of another's good — their work, their ease, their recognition — followed by a small souring of one's own. It arrives fast and is disowned faster — the emotion we most reliably feel and least reliably admit.
**What It Signals.** Envy reports desire by triangulation: something in *their* life is lighting up something unlived in *mine*. The other person is not the message; they are the mirror. Read precisely, envy is astonishingly specific — we envy only certain people certain things, and the pattern maps our own unbuilt life.
**Its Gradient.** [[Admiration]] → [[Longing]] → Envy → [[Covetousness]] → [[Resentment]]. Its neighbor [[Jealousy]] guards what is held; envy aches toward what is not.
**Its Gift.** A map of one's own values, drawn in ache. Envy points, with a compass's indifference to comfort, at what you are called to build. The [[Old World]] taught us to suppress the pointer or hate the person it pointed through; read truly, it is vocation knocking.
**Its Distortion.** Refused, it leaks out as subtle disparagement — the reflex that finds the flaw in every good thing others do. Enthroned, it becomes begrudging: no longer wanting the thing, but needing the other to lose it — the ache inverted into a wish for harm, which poisons the envier first.
**The Move.** Admit it, first to yourself — envy named loses half its acid. Then convert: ask the ache *what exactly do you want?* and translate the answer into your own next step on your own gradient. Bless the person who showed you; they carried your map. Aspiration is envy metabolized.
