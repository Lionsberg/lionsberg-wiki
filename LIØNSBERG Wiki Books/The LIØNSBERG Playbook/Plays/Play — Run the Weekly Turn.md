# Play — Run the Weekly Turn

*The Weekly Turn · a card of [[The LIØNSBERG Playbook]]*

*When* every week, same time. An hour or two; consistency matters more than length — the rhythm, kept, *is* the Game. One Play, composed of eight movements: 

**Arrive and Open** — Land together: a few breaths, a short silence, a word of intention. Remember who you are and why you play. Two minutes of real arrival beats twenty of drift.

**Check In** — A minute or two each: where am I, how am I arriving? No fixing, no commentary — witnessing only. This is how the Circle learns its weather.

**Learn Together** — A short shared nourishment: a passage from the Story, a Play, a question worth holding. Ten minutes; rotate who brings it.

**Weave the Dialogue** — *When* something real needs thinking together. Begin from *we do not know*. Speak from lived experience, when moved. Listen for resonance, not rebuttal. Build with *yes, and*. Own your triggers — negative emotion is often a gift naming something that longs to heal.

**Coordinate the Quest** — The working middle, thirty to forty minutes: What was done? What is blocked? What is needed? What are the Wise Right Next Steps? → [[Template For Project]] · [[LIØNSBERG Issue Tracker]]

**Make Commitments** — Each member names one specific, doable commitment for the week: *say what you will do.* Next week: *tell what you did; learn from the difference.* *(To forge: Commitment Tracker — until then, the `turns::` line on your Quest card serves.)*

**Take the Turn on the Board** — Two minutes before closing: a few lines from your Circle to the Whole — *did / will / learned*, plus needs and offers — posted with [[Template For Weekly Turn Post]] and kept on your Quest card for [[The Gameboard]]. The heartbeat of the Superorganism: skip it, and the body cannot feel you.

**Harvest and Close** — What are we learning? One appreciation or one improvement each. Gratitude round. A closing word. End on time — trust is built at the edges.

*These movements are proto-Patterns: the same elements recur across many Plays — the First Gathering, the Weekly Turn, the Turning. As they prove themselves in play, they will be articulated into [[The LIØNSBERG Pattern Language]] as its operational Patterns.*
