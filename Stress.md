# Stress
Stress is the reading of load approaching capacity — the system under tension that still holds.
**Felt Sense.** Tightening. Shoulders up, breath shallow and quick, attention narrowed to the load; the body mobilized — heart faster, senses sharpened, rest postponed. A bowstring drawn: stored force, not yet damage.
**What It Signals.** Stress reports the ratio of load to capacity: demand is rising toward what the instrument can carry. It is not the enemy of good work — it is the gauge on the side of the vessel. Read early, it says *choose: shed load, grow capacity, or ask the [[Circle]] for hands* — before the choice is made for you.
**Its Gradient.** [[Ease]] → [[Effort]] → [[Strain]] → Stress → [[Overwhelm]]. Its stalled neighbor is [[Stuck]]; its chronic shadow borders [[Resignation]].
**Its Gift.** Mobilization — and, in right dose, growth. Capacity expands under load it can absorb: muscles, minds, and communities all strengthen at the edge of strain, provided the load lifts and rest completes the cycle.
**Its Distortion.** Chronic stress — the emergency that never stands down. The mobilization designed for minutes runs for years; the gauge is read as a badge of importance. This is the [[Old World]]'s default operating state, and it burns the vessel to power the machine.
**The Move.** Complete the cycle. Discharge the mobilization through the body — move, breathe, shake, walk. Rest until the string actually slackens. Then renegotiate the load in daylight: what is shed, what is shared, what is truly yours. [[Sovereignty]] includes the sovereign right to carry less.
